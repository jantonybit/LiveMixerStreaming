1. Overview

LiveMixer Streaming is a virtual mixing console for Windows with low-latency ASIO. It brings together microphones (physical inputs of your interface), Windows audio (games, music, browser) and audio captured directly from specific applications, applies effects, and sends the result to an output called Master Out, ready for your streaming/recording software.

Core idea: audio you capture enters the mixer and does not come back out of the PC speakers. Your streaming program listens to the result through Master Out. This prevents echo and feedback.

2. Requirements

  • Windows 10 build 20348 or later (Windows 11 OK) — required for per-app capture.
  • An ASIO driver installed for your audio interface (e.g., UMC ASIO Driver). LiveMixer does not install drivers: it only uses what is already on the system.
  • The application is portable: everything stays inside the project folder. Nothing to install.
The ASIO SDK is distributed under GPLv3. Closed commercial use requires a license from Steinberg. Check the terms before distributing the executable.

3. Using two audio interfaces (the most common setup)

This is the most reliable way to separate what you hear/show from what you capture. Let us call the interfaces Board A and Board B:

  • Board A — motherboard onboard audio: handles Windows sounds, the game and the music player.
  • Board B — USB interface (e.g., Behringer UMC 204): handles the microphones (ASIO inputs) and the Master Out that goes to the stream.

Step by step

  1. In Windows, Settings → System → Sound → Output, choose Board A as the default output device. Games and players will use this board.
  2. In LiveMixer, select Board B's ASIO driver in ASIO Driver. Set Rate (48 kHz recommended) and Buffer (128 or 256; increase if you hear crackles).
  3. In PC Audio, choose Board A's output. LiveMixer will loopback that output and create the inputs PC AUDIO L and PC AUDIO R.
  4. Check Mute PC output. Board A audio is still captured at full level, but stops playing on the speakers. (The original Windows volume is restored when you stop the engine.)
  5. In Master Out, choose an output of Board B — for example OUT 1-2. Connect that output to your streaming software as an audio input.
  6. Set up the scene: in each channel use the Input combo:
    • IN 1, IN 2… = microphones on Board B (ASIO);
    • PC AUDIO L / PC AUDIO R = game/music coming from Board A;
    • <app> L / <app> R = a specific app captured directly (use Refresh apps to list them).
  7. If you capture apps directly, check Divert Windows apps so they stop playing on the speakers and go only to the mixer.
  8. Click START.
Golden rule: PC Audio and Master Out must never be the same device — the app blocks this automatically and shows FEEDBACK RISK if you try.
Signal flow:
Game/Windows (Board A) ──loopback──► PC AUDIO L/R ──┐ Specific app ──direct capture──► <app> L/R ───────────────────────┤ Microphone (Board B) ──ASIO IN──► IN 1 / IN 2 ───────────────────────┤ ▼ Channels → FX → Master │ ▼ Master Out (Board B) → Stream

5. Mixer channels

Each strip has, from top to bottom:

  • Name and Input (see the two-interface section).
  • Input meter, Gain (−60 to +18 dB) and Pan.
  • HPF — fixed high-pass filter at 80 Hz (12 dB/oct). Cuts rumble, handling noise and wind. Use on microphones and captures, but not on kick/bass.
  • Ø (phase) — inverts the channel polarity.
  • EQ — 4 bands (Low, Lo-Mid, Hi-Mid, High) with gain and frequency.
  • FX sends — one knob per effect (Delay, Reverb, Chorus), always post-fader. It sets how much of that channel goes to the effect; the effect name is shown above each knob.
  • Fader (−60 to +12 dB), M (mute) and S (solo).
Right-click any control to open MIDI Learn and assign a physical control.

6. FX rack

Located in the column to the left of the Master, it contains three fixed modules:

  1. Delay — Time, Feedback and Mix.
  2. Reverb (Schroeder) — Size, Damp and Mix.
  3. Chorus — Rate, Depth and Mix.

Each module has its own Return to the Master and a meter. Each effect's output goes straight to the Master, so different channels can send different amounts to the same effect.

Channel (post-fader) ──┬── Delay ──┐ ├── Reverb ──┼──► Master ──► Master Out └── Chorus ──┘

7. Master

The Master fader is on the far right, with stereo meters. A soft-clip limiter is always active to prevent digital distortion.

Meter colors

Green: up to 80% · Yellow: 81–93% · Red: above 94%.

8. MIDI Learn

  1. Open MIDI and make sure your controller shows as active.
  2. Right-click the desired mixer control (fader, knob, button).
  3. Move the matching control on your hardware. The binding appears in the list.
  4. If the command is already in use, the conflict dialog offers Replace, Use together or Cancel.

Buttons (mute, solo, HPF, phase) use latch mode: pressing toggles the state, releasing is ignored. Bindings are saved in config\midi.map and with each scene.

9. Scenes

A scene saves the whole console: faders, gain, pan, mute, solo, HPF, phase, EQ, FX sends, FX returns, effect parameters, Master and the MIDI map.

Not saved: ASIO/WASAPI routing, chosen devices, and apps' mute state (system items, not mix items).

10. Settings (gear icon, top right)

  • Language — Português or English. Control names and dialogs change instantly.
  • User manual — opens this manual in the chosen language (HTML in your browser).
  • Theme — Midnight, Gold, Ocean or Light.

Preferences are stored in config\ui.cfg. The interface automatically adapts to smaller screens (14" notebooks), wrapping lines and resizing panels.

11. Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to do
No ASIO driver appearsInstall your interface driver and click Refresh devices.
Crackles / noiseIncrease Buffer to 256/512 or lower the sample rate.
"FEEDBACK RISK"Choose different devices for PC Audio and Master Out, or check Mute PC output.
App not listedPlay some audio in the app and click Refresh apps (the list also refreshes by itself).
App audio still on speakersCheck Divert Windows apps.
Manual does not openMake sure the docs folder (or bin\docs) is next to the executable.
